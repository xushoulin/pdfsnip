How to extract pages from a PDF

  1. Open your file. Drag the PDF onto the box above or click to browse. It opens instantly on your device.
  2. Select pages. Click the page numbers, or type a range such as 2-5, 9. Ranges can be in any order.
  3. Extract and download. Click “Extract pages” — a new PDF with only the selected pages is saved to your device. The original file is untouched.

Extract vs. split: which one do you need?

Extracting copies the pages you choose into one new PDF and leaves the original unchanged — ideal when you need pages 5–8 of a 200-page contract. Splitting divides a PDF into several files, for example one file per page. If that is what you are after, use our Split PDF tool instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to extract pages from a PDF online?

With PDFSnip, yes — because there is no “online” step. The tool runs entirely in your browser and your file is never sent anywhere. It even works with your Wi-Fi switched off.

Can I extract pages from a scanned PDF?

Yes. Scanned PDFs are just images inside a PDF container, and pages are copied as-is, at original quality.

Can I extract non-consecutive pages, like pages 2, 5 and 9?

Absolutely. Click each page you want, or type 2, 5, 9 in the range box. Mixed formats like 1-3, 7, 10-12 work too.

Will bookmarks and links survive?

Page content, text and images are preserved perfectly. Document-level bookmarks usually reference the original structure and are not carried over. Internal links between extracted pages may stop working.

Does it work on iPhone, Android and Chromebook?

Yes. Any modern browser works — there is nothing to install. On mobile, the extracted PDF is saved to your Downloads or offered as a share target.
