How to Extract Pages from a PDF on Mac

macOS Preview has a hidden superpower: drag a thumbnail out, get a new PDF.

Every Mac ships with Preview, and Preview can do something most paid PDF editors advertise: pull individual pages out of a PDF with drag and drop. Here are the two best methods, and when to skip both.

Method 1: drag thumbnails out of Preview (the classic trick)

  1. Open the PDF in Preview. Double-click it — Preview is the default PDF viewer on macOS.
  2. Show the sidebar. Menu bar → View → Thumbnails (or press Cmd+Option+2).
  3. Select the pages you want. Click a thumbnail; hold Cmd to pick several non-adjacent pages, or Shift for a range.
  4. Drag them to your Desktop or a Finder folder. Preview instantly creates a new PDF containing exactly those pages.

The result is a genuine new PDF — original quality, no re-rendering, and form fields are preserved better than with browser print-to-PDF methods.

Method 2: print a page range from Preview

  1. Open the PDF in Preview and press Cmd+P.
  2. In the print dialog, find the Pages field and type your range, e.g. 2-6 or 1, 5, 9.
  3. Click the PDF dropdown in the bottom-left corner → Save as PDF.

This is faster when you know the exact page numbers, but like Chrome's method it re-renders pages, so scans can lose a little crispness and fillable forms may flatten.

Dragging works in reverse, too

The same thumbnail sidebar is a two-way street: you can drag a PDF file into Preview's sidebar to merge documents, or drag pages between two open documents. For heavy merging jobs though, a dedicated merge tool with reorder buttons is faster.

Limitations of the Preview workflow

  • It is Mac-only. Windows and Chromebook users need the Chrome print method or a web tool.
  • No page ranges across multiple documents — one PDF at a time.
  • No way to extract "every page matching a pattern" or batch-process many files.
  • If the PDF is hundreds of pages long, scrolling the thumbnail strip to find page 147 gets old fast.
